Interpretation

What your labs mean for the current medication, supplement, hair, and longevity stack.

What looks solid Metabolic health (glucose 83, best of the three draws — and more impressive than it looks, since none of the three were fasted), kidney function still improving (creatinine 1.09 → 0.95 → 0.91, eGFR 106 — and that is with 5 g creatine daily), liver enzymes flat on rosuvastatin (AST 24, ALT 22, ALP 49, bilirubin 0.6), electrolytes unremarkable, protein status good for wound healing (albumin 4.6, total protein 6.9), a normal white count with no left shift, thyroid (TSH 1.76), PSA (0.49), and a clean infectious screen.
New in the 13 Aug draw — three things changed, and the date matters
  • Lipids rose, and the comparison is like-for-like. LDL 76 → 91, triglycerides 65 → 114, non-HDL 90 → 112, with HDL up 50 → 60 so the ratio barely moved (2.8 → 2.9). Both this draw and May were non-fasting, so the direction is real rather than a fasting artifact — though non-fasting triglycerides swing more from day to day than fasted ones, so treat the size of the jump as softer than the direction. The GLP-1 taper explains it without needing anything else: about two weeks on a reduced 2.5 mg weaning dose, then 12 days off, so tirzepatide's triglyceride-lowering effect had been falling away for roughly a month with appetite and food quality following. Nothing here calls for a statin change.
  • Red cells fell proportionally, with pristine indices. Hemoglobin 15.5 → 14.4, hematocrit 46.1 → 42.0, RBC 4.96 → 4.68 — all down roughly 6–9% together, while MCV, MCH, MCHC, and RDW stayed normal. What that rules out is the thing worth ruling out: iron-restricted or otherwise impaired production would have pushed RDW up and MCV down, and neither moved. What it leaves is hydration state on the day plus ordinary variation over five and a half months, and there is nothing on record to pin it to more precisely than that — the pre-op fluid loading started 15 Aug, two days after this draw, so it is not the answer. One mechanism worth raising with Defy: angiotensin receptor blockers like olmesartan modestly suppress erythropoiesis, and that exposure is continuous. Everything is mid-range and none of it needed action.
  • This is a clean pre-TRT baseline — better than a post-op one would have been. Hematocrit 42.0% was measured before any surgical blood loss, so it is the number to compare against once testosterone starts. Testosterone reliably raises hematocrit and concern begins in the low 50s, so 42 is real headroom. Sequencing unchanged: Peptides & TRT.
  • Platelets 193 K/μL. Comfortably normal, and a reassuring number to have gone into a two-day procedure with. Also the raw material for PRP later — nothing here argues against it.
  • Because you do not fast, ApoB is the metric to ask for. Only the Jun Defy panel was fasted, and it carried no lipids. That is not a problem to fix by fasting — it is a reason to stop anchoring on LDL-C, which is a calculated number whose accuracy depends on the triglycerides that food moves most. ApoB counts atherogenic particles directly and is valid non-fasting; non-HDL (total minus HDL) needs no calculation and barely shifts after a meal, which makes your 90 → 112 the most trustworthy trend on the panel. Lp(a) is also fasting-independent and a once-in-a-lifetime measurement. Ask for ApoB plus Lp(a) at the recheck and you get a cleaner read than any fasted LDL-C would give you.
What needs improvement The Aug draw closed none of the gaps below — it repeated the CMP and lipids and added a CBC, but ferritin, vitamin D, and B12 were not on it. These remain the open questions:
  • Ferritin still unmeasured — and the CBC does not substitute for it. Normal MCV (89.7), normal MCH (30.8), and a low-normal RDW (11.6) do rule out iron-deficiency anemia, which is genuinely reassuring. They do not rule out depleted iron stores with a normal blood count, and that — iron deficiency without anemia — is precisely the pattern associated with shedding. Only a ferritin level answers it.
  • Androgen axis — most actionable. Free testosterone 9.7 pg/mL (lower third of range) and total T 390 ng/dL (low-normal for 45). Not deficient, but not optimal. A Defy conversation post-op makes sense before stacking more DIY androgen-adjacent supplements (boron, black maca). Mechanism read and sequencing: Peptides & TRT.
  • Vitamin D — dosing blind. You take D3+K2 daily without a 25-OH level. Not dangerous at typical doses, but you cannot optimize. Add at the next draw — see Not yet drawn.
  • Estradiol on the low side (<30 pg/mL, Feb 2026). Not a problem today, but worth rechecking if you add finasteride or pursue TRT — both can shift E2.
  • Creatinine context. 1.09 → 0.95 → 0.91 mg/dL with 5 g creatine daily and olmesartan — the trend is reassuring, but always disclose creatine when kidney labs are read.
  • Rising lipids need a clean recheck, not a reaction. See the 13 Aug summary above. The move is real, but it was measured at the end of a month-long GLP-1 taper — so the informative number is the one taken once Zepbound is back and eating has settled. Restoring that exposure should pull triglycerides back down, which makes plateau-or-dip a reasonable expectation rather than wishful thinking.
  • Optional refinements, not urgent: B12/folate not checked; ApoB/Lp(a) still not baselined, and now more worth having given the LDL and triglyceride move; DHEA-S 128 μg/dL (low-mid for age) — all reasonable next-draw adds if Defy is consolidating panels.
