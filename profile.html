Interpretation

What your labs mean for the current medication, supplement, hair, and longevity stack.

What looks solid Metabolic health (glucose 83, best of the three draws), kidney function still improving (creatinine 1.09 → 0.95 → 0.91, eGFR 106 — and that is with 5 g creatine daily), liver enzymes flat on rosuvastatin (AST 24, ALT 22, ALP 49, bilirubin 0.6), electrolytes unremarkable, protein status good for wound healing (albumin 4.6, total protein 6.9), a normal white count with no left shift, thyroid (TSH 1.76), PSA (0.49), and a clean infectious screen.
New in the Aug 2026 draw — the three things that changed
  • Lipids drifted up, all still in range. LDL 76 → 91, triglycerides 65 → 114, non-HDL 90 → 112. HDL also rose 50 → 60, so the cholesterol/HDL ratio barely moved (2.8 → 2.9). Two obvious peri-op drivers: Zepbound is paused, and the clinic sheet has you drinking two 20 oz Gatorades a day, which is a large daily sugar load — triglycerides are the most diet- and fasting-sensitive number on the panel. Treat this as a snapshot taken under unusual conditions, not a new baseline. Recheck 8–12 weeks after Zepbound resumes and normal eating returns, before anyone touches the statin dose.
  • You now have a pre-TRT hematocrit. Hematocrit 42.0% and hemoglobin 14.4 (down from 46.1 / 15.5 in Feb). Raising testosterone reliably pushes hematocrit up, and the usual point of concern is the low 50s — starting from 42 leaves real headroom, which makes the Defy conversation easier rather than harder. Sequencing unchanged: Peptides & TRT.
  • Platelets 193 K/μL. Comfortably normal, mid-range. Relevant only because PRP draws its effect from your own platelet concentration — nothing here argues against it.
What needs improvement The Aug draw closed none of the gaps below — it repeated the CMP and lipids and added a CBC, but ferritin, vitamin D, and B12 were not on it. These remain the open questions:
  • Ferritin still unmeasured — and the CBC does not substitute for it. Normal MCV (89.7), normal MCH (30.8), and a low-normal RDW (11.6) do rule out iron-deficiency anemia, which is genuinely reassuring. They do not rule out depleted iron stores with a normal blood count, and that — iron deficiency without anemia — is precisely the pattern associated with shedding. Only a ferritin level answers it.
  • Androgen axis — most actionable. Free testosterone 9.7 pg/mL (lower third of range) and total T 390 ng/dL (low-normal for 45). Not deficient, but not optimal. A Defy conversation post-op makes sense before stacking more DIY androgen-adjacent supplements (boron, black maca). Mechanism read and sequencing: Peptides & TRT.
  • Vitamin D — dosing blind. You take D3+K2 daily without a 25-OH level. Not dangerous at typical doses, but you cannot optimize. Add at the next draw — see Not yet drawn.
  • Estradiol on the low side (<30 pg/mL, Feb 2026). Not a problem today, but worth rechecking if you add finasteride or pursue TRT — both can shift E2.
  • Creatinine context. 1.09 → 0.95 → 0.91 mg/dL with 5 g creatine daily and olmesartan — the trend is reassuring, but always disclose creatine when kidney labs are read.
  • Rising lipids need a clean recheck, not a reaction. See the Aug summary above. The mistake to avoid is anchoring on a panel drawn mid-procedure with the GLP-1 paused.
  • Optional refinements, not urgent: B12/folate not checked; ApoB/Lp(a) still not baselined, and now more worth having given the LDL and triglyceride move; DHEA-S 128 μg/dL (low-mid for age) — all reasonable next-draw adds if Defy is consolidating panels.
